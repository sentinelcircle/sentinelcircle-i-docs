Common Problems

Most monitoring problems trace back to one of four things: a missing permission, monitoring not actually being on, the app not being on screen while iOS limits background activity, or a phone setting such as Low Power Mode, Background App Refresh, the silent switch or a Focus getting in the way. Check those first.

Best for: working out why an alert did not arrive, or why a sensor shows an error.

Details​

"I didn't get an alert I expected"​

  1. Is monitoring actually started? Check that the Status screen shows "ON". A configured sensor, channel and monitor do nothing on their own until you tap Start Monitoring.
  2. Are the sensor, channel and monitor all enabled? Adding an item doesn't automatically enable it, and the Free plan disables extra items beyond its limits. See Tiers & Upgrading.
  3. Check the Event Log (⋯ menu on the Status screen). It records what each channel did and when monitoring started and stopped, so it shows whether the alert was dispatched and what happened to each channel. It does not record individual sensor readings, so it cannot tell you why a sensor did not fire. It also starts empty again each time the app is closed and reopened.
  4. Check the alert delay. Wi-Fi and Bluetooth sensors wait before confirming a change, so a brief absence (a few seconds of a dropped signal) is deliberately ignored as noise. Even "No Debouncing" waits about a minute on Bluetooth. See Debounce.
  5. Was a radio switched off, or was the app out of the picture? If Wi-Fi or Bluetooth was turned off, or Airplane Mode is on, the matching sensor has no information and holds its last known state. It does not alert. That is deliberate: a radio you switched off is not evidence the person left. Turn the radio back on and check the sensor's row on the Status screen.
  6. Wi-Fi sensors only see the network the iPhone is joined to. Leaving is noticed when the phone joins a different Wi-Fi network. While it is on mobile data away from home, or Wi-Fi is off, the sensor holds and sends no alert; only a warning appears on the Status screen.
  7. A Test button works, but a real alert doesn't? The Test button in a channel's editor sends immediately, bypassing debounce and monitor state. It only proves the channel itself can deliver, not that the whole sensor → monitor → channel chain will fire under real conditions. A passing Test also does not promise delivery of a real alert: a silenced phone, a Focus or a closed app can still stop one.

"The alert came, but there was no sound" (or no notification)​

  • Notifications are off or blocked for Sentinel Circle: open Settings → Notifications → Sentinel Circle and allow them. The Status screen warns "Sounds for Sentinel Circle notifications are off in Settings. Alerts will be silent." when sounds are off.
  • The silent switch is on. An alert is a Time Sensitive notification. It can break through a Focus but not the silent switch, so unless Apple has granted the app the Critical Alerts permission, an alert does not sound while the iPhone is silenced. The Status screen then warns "Alerts will not sound while this device is silenced."
  • A Focus is on. Check that Sentinel Circle is allowed in your Focus, and that Time Sensitive notifications are allowed for the app in Settings → Notifications.

"Monitoring stopped, or sensors paused, when I left the app"​

iOS limits what apps can do in the background, and these are the usual causes:

  • The app was swiped away in the app switcher. This stops monitoring. Open Sentinel Circle again and monitoring starts by itself.
  • The iPhone restarted. Sentinel Circle does not start by itself after a restart. Unlock the iPhone and open the app.
  • Low Power Mode is on, or Background App Refresh is off for Sentinel Circle. Check Settings → Battery and Settings → General → Background App Refresh. The Status screen can show a "Background activity restricted" warning. See Power & Battery.
  • Sentinel Circle is not on the screen. Wi-Fi, Bluetooth and Sentinel Area sensors check most reliably while the app is open on the screen, and some pause until you return to it. For a phone that monitors around the clock, plug it in, turn on Keep Screen On and leave the app open (see Monitoring Settings).

"A sensor shows an error or never resolves"​

Usually a missing permission, or a radio that is switched off. Open Settings → Permissions. It counts the permissions your current configuration needs and opens the setup wizard, where each one has its own button. A sensor missing a required permission can't determine its state at all, and neither can one whose radio is off. If iOS has permanently refused a permission, the wizard's button opens Sentinel Circle's page in the Settings app instead, where you can allow it.

"Wi-Fi or Bluetooth scanning isn't finding anything"​

  • Location: iOS requires Location access to read the name of the Wi-Fi network the phone is joined to, and to notice a Bluetooth beacon, even though Sentinel Circle never records where you are. Choose "Allow While Using App" and leave Precise Location on. The iPhone's main Location Services switch (Settings → Privacy & Security → Location Services) must be on as well.
  • Bluetooth: Bluetooth access must be allowed for Sentinel Circle (choose "Allow" when iOS asks), and Bluetooth turned on (in Settings or Control Center). When scanning for a beacon to select, move the phone close to it. A new beacon is often asleep: remove its pull tab, press its button, or check the maker's manual.
  • Airplane Mode turns both radios off. With a radio off, the matching sensor holds its state instead of alerting.

See The Setup Wizard & Permissions.

"I can't change settings"​

  • Monitoring is on. Settings opens in View Only Mode ("Disarm monitoring to make changes"). Stop monitoring first.
  • The app is locked. See the next entry.

"I can't get into Settings: the app is locked"​

If App Lock is on, tap the Unlock App button and enter your password. That button is the password route, not the lock icon. The lock icon is the emergency route: tap it 7 times within 5 seconds (unless you turned off emergency unlock, in which case the icon opens the password box directly). If emergency unlock is off and you have forgotten the password, it cannot be recovered, and deleting and reinstalling the app does not remove the lock. See App Lock.

"I bought the upgrade but the app still says Free"​

Open the upgrade dialog from the Upgrade to Standard card and tap Restore Purchases, with the Apple ID that made the purchase. If it said "Waiting for approval", Standard unlocks once the purchase is approved. See Tiers & Upgrading.

Still stuck?​

Check each relevant page under Sensors and Channels. Most list their specific permission requirements.
